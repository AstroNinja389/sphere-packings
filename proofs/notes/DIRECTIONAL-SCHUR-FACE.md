# Holding the exact Fourier origin price while repairing a harmonic kernel

For a positive definite constant harmonic \(G\) and a nonzero origin column \(z\), set \(s=z^TG^{-1}z\), \(C=zz^T/s\). Cauchy–Schwarz in the \(G\) inner product gives \(G-C\succeq0\). For \(0<\theta\le1\), take \(G_\theta=C+\theta(G-C)\). It is positive definite; \(G_\theta G^{-1}z=z\), hence \(z^TG_\theta^{-1}z=s\) exactly. The matrix \((1-\theta)(G-C)\) has become available for the other harmonic degrees. Shrinking the entire origin by \(\theta\) would instead multiply the cost by \(1/\theta\).

With the full sum of harmonic matrices fixed as an upper bound in Loewner order, search for nonnegative harmonic matrices that make the off-diagonal pair sums nonpositive on each saturated lattice plane. A successful factorization must pass both geometric domains with exact rational coefficients. Rational factor rounding and the finished Fourier/population proof are independently checked. The identity above by itself gives no density estimate. The first new test targets the four-feature rank-32 discovery kernel, whose sampled objective alone had overstated its benefit.

In the stronger variable-matrix version, impose a rational lower bound (G'_0\succeq\theta C+\varepsilon(G-C)) with positive \(\theta,\varepsilon\). Optimize the remaining part of the zero harmonic jointly with all the nonconstant harmonics. The inverse cost in the original direction is at most \(s/\theta\) by inverse monotonicity. The other matrix degrees remain positive semidefinite and their sum fits inside the original total matrix budget. With four radial features in dimension 32, the final \(\theta=997/1000\) kernel passed the full two-line and three-line sign tests after 22 exact-witness exchange rounds. Its density calculation separately reoptimizes the Fourier polynomial and the common parity population; the final proof evaluates the actual inverse without using the conservative cost cap.

# Lowering the origin cost while preserving each diagonal value

Write a compact pair kernel as \(\sum_{j\ge0}H(a)^tA_jH(b)G_{2j}^{(n)}(u)\), with the old normalization absorbed into the matrices. If its source dimension exceeds \(n\), expand the even zonal polynomials in dimension \(n\) before doing this. For a fixed origin column \(z\) and overall coefficient \(\alpha>0\), the charge in the spatial bound is \(z^tA_0^{-1}z/\alpha\). The diagonal polynomial depends on the matrices only through \(\sum A_j\), because every normalized zonal polynomial equals one at one.

Choose rational positive semidefinite matrices \(A'_j\) with the same sum, with \(A'_0\) positive definite. Prove both the isolated-pair and three-line plane signs of the new kernel on the full physical domains. Then the old radial, parity, Fourier and tail proof still applies verbatim to the spatial polynomial. Its allowance improves by precisely \(z^t(A_0^{-1}-{A'_0}^{-1})z/\alpha\) when this is positive. A reciprocal branch keeps its actual denominator gain and its prior complementary bound.

The search decomposes individual nonconstant harmonic matrices into rational rank-one matrices and varies their allocations over several harmonics, now including degree zero. For any vector \(v\), the affine lower bound \(z^tM^{-1}z\ge 2v^tz-v^tMv\) supplies a cut to the origin minimization. The exact verifier instead computes both origin costs, checks the common diagonal as a rational matrix identity, and rechecks the physical signs on their entire domains. Its computation of the old constant harmonic uses spherical moments directly. A floating-point optimum or a saved optimization status supplies no part of the proof. `verify_origin_saving.py`

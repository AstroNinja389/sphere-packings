# Extending the compact plane inequality through squared length two

Normalize the lattice to minimum squared length one. Choose \(1<R\le2\). In each saturated lattice plane, at most three opposite pairs have squared length strictly below \(R\). Indeed no vector that short belongs to twice the plane lattice. Two distinct opposite pairs in the same parity class would have two nonzero half-sums with squared lengths adding to \((a+b)/2<2\), which is impossible.

Any two of the retained vectors generate the entire plane lattice. Their independent classes modulo two give odd index. Their parallelogram has area at most \(\sqrt{ab}<2\), while a minimum-one plane lattice has covolume at least \(\sqrt3/2\). For the latter fact take a shortest vector as the first basis element and reduce the other basis element along it to a coefficient of absolute value at most one half. The reduced vector is at least as long as the shortest, so the height is at least \(\sqrt3/2\) times its length. The index is therefore less than three, and must be one.

With three retained lines, every pair has index one. Thus in a basis consisting of the first two vectors the third vector has both coefficients of absolute value one. The squared cosine for the pair of squared lengths \(a,b\) is \((a+b-c)^2/(4ab)\), where \(c\) is the remaining squared length. With only two retained lines the shorter signed sum has squared length at least \(R\). The squared cosine is at most \((a+b-R)^2/(4ab)\).

For a common full support \(R\), set \(h(a)=(R-a)_+/(R-1)\) and take features \(h^b,h^{b+s},h^{b+2s}\) for positive integers \(b,s\). Order the three squared lengths and write their radial coordinates as \(x,xy,xyz\), with \(0\le x,y,z\le1\). After removing the common factor \(x^{2b}y^b\), the pair weights are respectively \(1,z^b,(yz)^b\). The two-line cube uses \(x,xy\) and a third coordinate multiplying its squared-cosine cap. Continuity handles faces where a radial feature vanishes. Clearing strictly positive length denominators and checking the resulting rational polynomials throughout both cubes proves the off-diagonal sign required by the compact kernel.

Each source using this sharper two-line cap uses its entire support. Changing the cutoff on an existing source can bring a previously retained third line outside the support and changes the two-line condition. Scaling all squared lengths by a common factor at least one is allowed because the resulting lattice still has minimum at least one.

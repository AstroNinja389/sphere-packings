# Closing an interval of possible dual minima

Use the notation of the [section reduction](SECTION-TRANSFER.md). In particular $u$ is the squared minimum of the balanced rank-ten dual, $e=\det Q$ and $T=Q^{-1}$ in rank nine. Balancing gives $u^5d\le1024$, and $e=ud$. On an interval $l\le u\le h$ the determinant ceiling can therefore be tightened to $\min(1110.28,1133h,1024/l^4)$, omitting the last term for $l=0$. The marking gives $e\ge\det P$ by AM–GM. A lower bound on $d$ is 768 on the ten exceptional marks and 1113 on the others, by the cited rank-eleven certificate. In intervals with $h\le9799/10000$ the direct orientation is forced. The [interval checker](audit_cells.py) uses exact rational endpoints and discards an interval only after a contradiction.

For a single projected integer direction let $b=T[z]$. The rank-two projection inequality gives $b\ge 3u/4$. In the direct orientation the first normal, second normal and lift of this direction have squared Gram–Schmidt lengths $v,u/s,b/s$. The first two are shortest in their corresponding lattices. Size reduction gives a rank-three KZ basis, so $b/s\ge 2v/3$. Multiplying the two bounds yields $b^2\ge suv/2=s^{11}e/(2E)\ge e/(2E)$. Here $e=ud,\ d=vE/s^{10}$ and $s\ge1$. This proof also works when the chosen direction generates a proper sublattice.

For two independent integer directions form the reciprocal Gram matrix $B=ZTZ^t$. Their lifts and the shortest rank-ten normal generate a rank-three lattice of determinant $u\det B$ and squared minimum at least $u$. The rank-three Hermite constant cubed is two, so $\det B\ge u^2/2$. In the direct orientation take a reduced basis of the projected plane in the rank-eleven dual, and lift it together with the first two normals. Denote the four squared Gram–Schmidt lengths by $v,u/s,c_3,c_4$. The first normal is shortest, the second is shortest after projection, and the first plane basis vector is shortest in the projected plane. The three-dimensional KZ bound applied successively to positions 1–3 and 2–4 gives $c_3\ge 2v/3,\ c_4\ge 2u/(3s)$. Thus $\det B=s^2c_3c_4\ge4suv/9\ge4e/(9E)$. [Three-dimensional KZ constant, equation (1.3)](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/files/2066021/Metis197411.pdf) · [Companion paper on section projections](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2829)

For the three directions $z,w,z\pm w$, the sum of squared lengths is $\operatorname{tr}(HB)$ with $H=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&\pm1\\\pm1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The bound $\operatorname{tr}(HB)\ge2\sqrt{\det H\,\det B}$ gives an additional lower bound $\sqrt{12\det B}$. The same inequality applies to every positive definite rational two-by-two price matrix used in the [plane verifier](audit_planes.py).

Finally $T=\sum_i w_i x_i x_i^t$ on the minimum pairs of the marking. The rank-one inequality $T\succeq w_i x_i x_i^t$ implies $w_i Q[x_i]\le1$, so $0\le w_i\le1/4$. Since the weights sum to at most $9/4$, the weighted sum of any nonnegative contact evaluations is bounded above by one quarter of the sum of the nine largest. The auditor checks this on the whole contact set before applying the determinant contradiction. Both interval auditors require adjacent intervals to cover all admissible $u$ before removing a marking. A partially covered interval supplies no whole-family exclusion.

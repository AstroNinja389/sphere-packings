# Dimension 14. Short parity frames and a common dual normal coordinate

Let D be Gram determinant with squared minimum four. The packing density is V/√D, V=π⁷/5040. The external sharp rank-twelve theorem gives the rank-thirteen minimum-four determinant lower bound A=(729/4)^(13/11), by Mordell. We use only this lower bound on *every* rank-thirteen lattice, and 729 in rank twelve. The present computation does not rerun the large external census. [Theorem 17 and Remark 22](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2825)

## The geometric setup

Normalize L to squared minimum one, and take k distinct opposite pairs represented by x_i in one class modulo 2L. In Λ=√2 L let f_i=x_i/√2 and δ_i=(|x_i|²−2)/2. Then f_i±f_j belong to Λ and are nonzero. Their squared lengths are at least two. The Gram Q of the f_i has diagonal 1+δ_i, off-diagonal absolute values at most (δ_i+δ_j)/2, and every δ_i+δ_j≥0. Let m=Σmax(0,δ_i). The integer even-sum lattice Γ=D_k in these coordinates belongs to Λ. Write M for the comparison matrix with the same diagonal and most-negative allowed off-diagonals. Whenever M is positive definite, Q is positive definite, det Q≥det M and |(Q⁻¹)ij|≤(M⁻¹)ij. To see this, divide out the diagonal; the normalized M is I−B with B entrywise nonnegative of spectral radius below one. The geometric series gives the inverse inequality, and the trace power series for log det gives the determinant inequality. Hadamard gives det Q≤exp(m).

Under a bound m≤m₀, sorting the positive δ gives a simplex with vertices zero and k-tail step vectors of height m₀/k for each possible tail length k. If δ₁<0, sort −δ₁≤δ₂≤… instead and impose the mass budget on the last coordinates. Its vertices in the original coordinates are among the same list and (−m₀/(k−1),m₀/(k−1),…,m₀/(k−1)). Positivity and determinant lower bounds extend from vertices by convexity and log concavity. A bound on gᵀM⁻¹g extends by the Schur complement. The replay checks each vertex using actual principal minors and determinant increments for the directions e_i and e_i+e_j. This controls both signs for Q⁻¹.

Let U be the primitive closure of Γ in its span, j=[U:Γ] and G=U/Γ. Its covolume is 2√det Q/j. For g∈G let χ_i(g) be its i-th coordinate modulo one. Every order-two element has a nonzero binary support of even weight at least eight in the mass ranges used below. Indeed an integral nonzero glue class contains a unit coordinate vector. A half-support of even weight 4 or 6 has representatives ±1/2 with fixed sign parity. Their mean squared norm is at most (w+m)/4<2; their signs are pairwise uncorrelated. Weight two also gives a representative below two by the off-diagonal bound. These arguments may increase the negative diagonal excess to zero. In rank 13 the binary rank is at most two: seven nonzero words of a three-dimensional subcode would have total weight at least 56, while any coordinate appears at most four times, giving at most 52. Thus the number t of self-inverse characters is at most four.

## Thirteen pairs and circles

The square of the covolume of Λ is D/2¹⁴. Every nonzero vector of its dual has squared norm at least c=2A/D: for a primitive normal the determinant of its rank-thirteen perpendicular section is its squared norm times det Λ, and that section has minimum at least two. Nonprimitive multiples satisfy the same lower bound.

Suppose k=13 and let h be the layer spacing above U, so h=j√D/(256√det Q). One lift generating Λ/U fixes a single circle phase θ_i for each e_i. Explicitly, if its section offset is a in the f-basis, take θ_i=−a_i modulo one. For any signed difference with zero character, its shortest dual lift has norm squared equal to its Q⁻¹ norm plus the squared circle distance divided by h². This also holds for individual coordinates of trivial character. The signed coordinate labels are distinct formal labels, and all use this same map. Partition the labels by character.

If all pair norms are ≤B₂, two distinct coordinate indices whose oriented characters agree must be spaced by at least h√(c−B₂). For a non-self-inverse pair of characters, orient all corresponding indices to the same character and use the unit circle. For a self-inverse character both signs land together, so the phase can be folded onto [0,1/2]; distinct indices must still be separated. In the zero-character bin the phases must also be at least h√(c−B₁) from zero, where B₁ bounds the individual norms. No spacing between opposite copies of the same index is needed.

The release evaluates the circle inequalities at ten cutoffs for the largest squared length, each at D ≤ 314. For a non-self-inverse character the unit circle holds at most max(1, floor(1/(js))) labels. A nonzero self-inverse character holds at most 1+floor(1/(2js)) folded labels. The zero bin holds at most max(0,1+floor((1/2−jr)/(js))). Here r and s are the certified singleton and pair spacings before multiplication by j. The verifier compares their squares in directed arithmetic and checks every allowed j and t. [Ten certified rows](../rank14-adaptive-314.json) · [Vertex and capacity checks](../rank14_adaptive.py)

## Twelve pairs and a flat two-torus

For k=12 and m≤1/20, j≤5 by 729 in rank twelve. The projected normal lattice has a two-dimensional dual period lattice N. Its area is 256√det Q/(j√D). Choose two actual extension generators. For every coordinate e_i their offsets give a point z_i modulo N by solving their two linear pairing equations. Signed differences with equal character lift to Λ* exactly as in the circle case. N itself has minimum squared length at least c. Put R²=c−B₂. Distinct indices in each character bin give points at least R apart on this flat torus; include the origin in the zero bin. Opposite labels of the same index can violate this spacing only in a self-inverse bin. Such a pair is within R/2 of one of the four points of order dividing two. Two different indices cannot have the same such point. In the zero bin the origin is unavailable because each coordinate there must itself be at least R from zero. Removing one point from every conflicting pair leaves at least 26−4t points across j tori. Their periodic lifts are ordinary planar packings. The planar packing theorem requires area at least √3 R²/2 per point. For t≤2 this implies
$$
18\sqrt3(2A-B_2D)\le512\sqrt D\,\sqrt{\det Q}.
$$
At D≤318, B₂≤3350/1669 and det Q≤exp(1/20) contradict this inequality by a positive margin larger than 18. The singleton and coincident opposite-label cases are included by the deletion rule.

The remaining case j=t=4 is an elementary binary glue. All three nonzero words have weight eight, since each of the twelve coordinates appears at most twice. There are three blocks of four corresponding to the three binary column types. In the undeformed reference, U consists of triples from (D₄*)³ with sum of the three discriminant labels zero in D₄*/D₄. To see this, D₁₂ already imposes the even-sum parity on the integer block labels. Half-block labels occupy an even number of blocks. Their integer parity correction is a linear functional on this two-dimensional binary code because doubling a four-entry half-block lies in D₄. It can be canceled by choosing a coordinate sign change in suitable blocks.

This reference U has squared covering radius at most 3/2. Round each block to D₄* with residual length r_i. The 24 unit vectors consist of three orthonormal frames of opposite pairs. In each frame the residual coordinates have absolute values ≤1/2 and sum of absolute values ≤1, from the reference Voronoi inequalities for those unit vectors and their signed half-sums. Thus r_i²≤1/2. A wrong label sum can be corrected in the block of largest residual. For the required nonzero label one frame is available; its largest absolute residual coordinate is at least r_i². The increase is at most 1−2r_i², giving total ≤1+max r_i²≤3/2. **torus_check.py** verifies all frame and signed half-sum identities. In Q the covering radius squared is therefore ≤(3/2)[1+(1+√12)m/2], since the top eigenvalue of Q−I is bounded by that of (δ⁺1ᵀ+1δ⁺ᵀ)/2. The primitive normal projection has minimum squared length at least two minus this cover. Its planar Gram determinant is at least three quarters the square of that minimum. As det Q≥1, multiplication by the section determinant gives D>339 for m≤1/20, and D≥768 at the undeformed frame. This exhausts all groups. The resulting twelve-pair excess exceeds 1/10 whenever D≤318.

## Fifteen pairs

For fifteen vectors in dimension fourteen their Gram matrix has rank at most fourteen.
